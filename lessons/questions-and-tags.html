Questions, embedded questions and tag questions

Questions follow a fixed word order. The ECL often tests four traps: the missing auxiliary, subject questions, embedded questions that wrongly keep question word order, and the tag at the end of a sentence.

The auxiliary comes before the subject. When be is the main verb, simply invert it: "Is he ready?" With other verbs in the present simple or the past simple, add do / does / did and use the base form.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Where you went yesterday?✓Right: Where did you go yesterday?
  • ✗Wrong: Does he works here?✓Right: Does he work here?

Subject questions (no do)

When who or what is the subject of the question, there is no do / does / did, and the word order is the same as in a statement.

  • Who called you? (Someone called you.)
  • What happened at the gate?
  • Compare: Who did you call? (You called someone.)

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Who did call you?✓Right: Who called you?

Embedded (indirect) questions

After Do you know…? Can you tell me…? I wonder… I don't know… Could you explain…?, the question becomes part of a sentence. It takes statement word order (subject + verb), with no do / does / did.

Direct questionEmbedded question
Where is the office?Can you tell me where the office is?
What time does the bus leave?Do you know what time the bus leaves?
Why did he quit?I wonder why he quit.
Is she married?I don't know if / whether she is married.

ECL tipThe question mark depends on how the sentence starts: "Do you know where he is?" but "I wonder where he is."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Do you know where is the station?✓Right: Do you know where the station is?

Tag questions

A tag question checks information or asks for agreement. A positive sentence takes a negative tag, and a negative sentence takes a positive tag. Use the same auxiliary as the sentence (or do / does / did) and a pronoun.

  • You're a pilot, aren't you?
  • She doesn't smoke, does she?
  • They left early, didn't they?
  • He can swim, can't he?
  • We haven't met, have we?

So do I, neither do I

To agree with…UseExample
a positive sentenceso + auxiliary + subject"I'm tired." "So am I." · "She likes coffee." "So do I."
a negative sentenceneither / nor + auxiliary + subject"I can't swim." "Neither can I." · "He didn't call." "Nor did she."

To disagree, use the subject + auxiliary: "I like it." "I don't." · "I didn't go." "I did."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: "I like pizza." "So I do."✓Right: "I like pizza." "So do I."
