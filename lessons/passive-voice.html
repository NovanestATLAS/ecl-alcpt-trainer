The passive voice: active to passive in every tense

In an active sentence, the subject does the action. In a passive sentence, the subject receives the action. Use the passive when the action matters more than the person who does it, or when you don't know who did it.

How to make the passive

Passive = be + past participle (V3)

the object of the active verb becomes the subject · be takes the tense of the active verb

Only verbs that have an object (transitive verbs) can be passive.

  • Active: The mechanics repair the trucks.
  • Passive: The trucks are repaired by the mechanics.

Verbs with no object (happen, arrive, die, sleep, occur, exist) cannot be passive.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The bridge was build in 1990.✓Right: The bridge was built in 1990.
  • ✗Wrong: The accident was happened at night.✓Right: The accident happened at night.

The passive in every tense

TenseActivePassive
present simpleThey clean the rooms.The rooms are cleaned.
present progressiveThey are cleaning the rooms.The rooms are being cleaned.
present perfectThey have cleaned the rooms.The rooms have been cleaned.
past simpleThey cleaned the rooms.The rooms were cleaned.
past progressiveThey were cleaning the rooms.The rooms were being cleaned.
past perfectThey had cleaned the rooms.The rooms had been cleaned.
future simpleThey will clean the rooms.The rooms will be cleaned.
future perfectThey will have cleaned the rooms.The rooms will have been cleaned.
be going toThey are going to clean the rooms.The rooms are going to be cleaned.

The perfect progressive tenses (have been cleaning) are almost never used in the passive.

Passive with modals, infinitives and -ing

FormExample
modal + be + V3The form must be signed by your commander.
modal + have been + V3The letter should have been sent yesterday.
to be + V3Nobody likes to be interrupted.
being + V3He hates being told what to do.

By + agent, and verbs with two objects

Add by + the doer only when it is important or new information. Leave it out when the doer is obvious, unknown or not important.

  • My car was damaged last night. (We don't know who damaged it.)
  • "Hamlet" was written by Shakespeare. (The writer is important.)

Verbs such as give, send, show, offer, tell, teach have two objects, so they have two possible passives.

  • Active: They gave the recruits new boots.
  • Passive: The recruits were given new boots. (more common)
  • Passive: New boots were given to the recruits.

It is said that… / He is believed to…

With verbs such as say, believe, think, know, report, expect and consider, formal English uses two passive patterns.

ActiveIt + passive + that…Subject + passive + to-infinitive
People say that he is rich.It is said that he is rich.He is said to be rich.
People believe that the storm has passed.It is believed that the storm has passed.The storm is believed to have passed.
They expect that the talks will fail.It is expected that the talks will fail.The talks are expected to fail.

ECL tipUse to have + V3 when the action happened before the reporting: "He is thought to have escaped last night."

The get-passive, and "have something done"

In spoken English, get + V3 can replace be for things that happen to someone, often by accident: "He got injured during the exercise." · "Our car got stolen."

To say that someone does a service for you (I had my car repaired), see the lesson on the causative: have and get something done.

How the ECL and ALCPT test the passive

Passive items give you the time of the action and ask for the right form of be plus the past participle. Find the tense clue (yesterday, right now, already, by Friday), put be in that tense, and add the past participle of the main verb.

Practice itemAnswerWhy
The bridge ___ last year.was builtPast simple passive: was / were + past participle.
The new barracks ___ right now.are being paintedPresent continuous passive: am / is / are being + past participle.
All the reports must ___ by Friday.be finishedAfter a modal: be + past participle.
The letter ___ yet.hasn't been sentYet points to the present perfect: has / have been + past participle.

Use the passive when the action matters more than who did it, or when you do not know who did it: "The base was attacked at night."

Tricky cases

A few verbs and patterns trap learners every time.

  • Verbs with no object have no passive: happen, arrive, die, occur, seem. The accident happened at noon.
  • Be born is always passive: I was born in 2001.
  • By names the person who acts; with names the tool: The lock was opened by the mechanic with a small key.
  • Passive questions put be or the auxiliary first: When was the base built? Has the order been given?

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The accident was happened at noon.✓Right: The accident happened at noon.
  • ✗Wrong: The report was wrote by the captain.✓Right: The report was written by the captain.
  • ✗Wrong: English is speak here.✓Right: English is spoken here.
