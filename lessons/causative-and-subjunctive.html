The causative (have something done) and the subjunctive (insist that he go)

The causative says that you arrange, persuade or force someone else to do something: I had my car repaired, They had him wait, I got him to help me. The subjunctive is the base form of the verb after verbs such as insist and suggest: He insists that she be present. Both are tested on the ECL because the verb form looks wrong at first sight.

The causative verbs at a glance

StructureMeaningExample
have + person + base verbask or arrange for someone to do it (often their job)I had the mechanic check the brakes.
get + person + to + verbpersuade someone to do itI got my brother to help me.
make + person + base verbforce someone to do itThe sergeant made us run five miles.
let + person + base verballow someone to do itThe captain let us leave early.
have / get + thing + past participlesomeone does a service for youI had my hair cut. · We got the truck repaired.

Have something done

have / get + object + past participle (V3)

the tense goes on have: has, had, is having, will have, has had…

You don't do the action yourself: someone does it for you, usually a service you pay for.

  • I have my car serviced every six months.
  • She is having her uniform cleaned.
  • We had the windows replaced last year.
  • You should get your eyes tested.
  • Where did you have your photos taken?

The same structure can describe something bad that happened to you: "He had his wallet stolen on the train."

ECL tipWatch the word order: have + object + V3. "I had my hair cut" means a barber cut it. "I had cut my hair" is the past perfect: I cut it myself, earlier.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I had repaired my car at the garage.✓Right: I had my car repaired at the garage.
  • ✗Wrong: I cut my hair yesterday at the barbershop.✓Right: I had my hair cut yesterday at the barbershop.

Have someone do, get someone to do

have + person + base verb

no to

Ask someone to do something, often as part of their job, or because you have authority.

  • The commander had the guards check every vehicle.
  • I'll have my assistant send you the file.

get + person + to + verb

with to

Persuade or convince someone to do something.

  • I finally got him to stop smoking.
  • Can you get the driver to wait for us?

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I got him clean the room.✓Right: I got him to clean the room.
  • ✗Wrong: She had him to wait outside.✓Right: She had him wait outside.

Make and let

make + person + base verb

no to in the active · to in the passive

Force or cause someone to do something.

  • The instructor made us repeat the exercise.
  • Onions make me cry.
  • We were made to repeat the exercise. (passive)

let + person + base verb

no to · no passive: use be allowed to

Allow someone to do something.

  • My parents let me drive their car.
  • We were allowed to leave early. (not we were let leave)

Similar verbs take to: allow, permit, force, cause, persuade, encourage. Help can take the base verb or to: "She helped me (to) carry the boxes."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The sergeant made us to run.✓Right: The sergeant made us run.
  • ✗Wrong: They let me to go.✓Right: They let me go.

The subjunctive: insist that he go

After verbs and expressions of demand, suggestion, recommendation or necessity, American English uses that + subject + the base form of the verb, for every person and in every tense. There is no -s and no past form, and the negative is not + base verb.

After these verbs……and these expressions
insist, demand, require, request, order, commandit is essential / vital / necessary / important that…
suggest, recommend, propose, advise, urge, askit is required / recommended / imperative / crucial that…
  • The captain insists that every soldier be on time.
  • The doctor suggested that he rest for a week.
  • They demanded that the prisoner be released.
  • It is essential that she not miss the briefing.
  • I recommend that he take the advanced course.

British English often uses should here ("He insists that she should be present"). The ECL tests the American form, with the base verb.

When insist means "say firmly that something is true", it takes a normal tense: "He insisted that he was innocent." The base form is for demands: "He insisted that she be present."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: The officer demanded that he leaves.✓Right: The officer demanded that he leave.
  • ✗Wrong: I suggest that he goes.✓Right: I suggest that he go.
  • ✗Wrong: The officer requested that we didn't talk.✓Right: The officer requested that we not talk.

Insist on doing, insist that…

Insist has two patterns.

PatternExample
insist on + -ing or a nounHe insisted on paying for dinner. · She insists on punctuality.
insist on + someone's + -ing (formal)The sergeant insisted on our wearing helmets.
insist that + subject + base verbThe sergeant insisted that we wear helmets.

Suggest and recommend work the same way: + -ing, or + that + subject + base verb. Never + person + to-infinitive.

  • I suggest taking a taxi. · I suggest that you take a taxi.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He insisted to pay.✓Right: He insisted on paying.

Would rather and it's time

StructureMeaningExample
would rather + base verbprefer (same subject)I'd rather stay home tonight.
would rather + person + past simpleprefer someone else to do itI'd rather you stayed here. (now or in the future)
it's (high) time + subject + past simpleit should happen nowIt's time we left. · It's high time you got a haircut.
