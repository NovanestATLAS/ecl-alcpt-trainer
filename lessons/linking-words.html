Linking words: conjunctions and discourse markers

Linking words connect ideas and show how they relate: addition, contrast, cause, result, purpose or time. The ECL often tests the grammar that follows them: a clause (subject + verb) or a noun or -ing form.

Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS)

For, and, nor, but, or, yet, so join two equal parts. Put a comma before them when they join two complete sentences.

ConjunctionMeaningExample
andadditionHe cleaned the truck, and she checked the radio.
but, yetcontrastThe test was hard, but I passed.
orchoiceYou can take the bus or walk.
soresultIt was raining, so we stayed inside.
forreason (formal)He was tired, for he had worked all night.
nornegative addition (inverted order)He doesn't smoke, nor does he drink.
both… and, either… or, neither… nor, not only… but alsopairsNeither the captain nor the sergeant was there.

With either… or and neither… nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: "Neither the soldiers nor the officer is ready."

Contrast

+ clause (subject + verb)+ noun or -ingBetween two sentences
although, even though, thoughdespite, in spite ofHowever, … · Nevertheless, … · Even so, …
while, whereas (a comparison)despite the fact that + clauseOn the other hand, …
  • Although it was raining, we continued the march.
  • Despite the rain, we continued the march.
  • In spite of being tired, he finished the run.
  • It was raining. However, we continued the march.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Despite it was raining, we went out.✓Right: Despite the rain, we went out. (or Although it was raining, we went out.)
  • ✗Wrong: Although it was raining, but we went out.✓Right: Although it was raining, we went out.

Cause and result

Cause + clauseCause + noun or -ingResult
because, as, sincebecause of, due to, owing to, on account ofso, therefore, as a result, consequently, thus, that's why
  • The flight was canceled because the weather was bad.
  • The flight was canceled because of the bad weather.
  • The weather was bad. Therefore, the flight was canceled.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Because of it was late, we took a taxi.✓Right: Because it was late, we took a taxi.

Purpose

Linking wordFollowed byExample
to, in order to, so as tobase verbHe studies hard in order to pass the ECL.
in order not to, so as not tobase verbWe left early so as not to miss the bus.
so thatsubject + can / could / will / wouldSpeak louder so that everyone can hear you.
fora noun (or -ing for the use of a thing)We stopped for lunch. · This knife is for cutting rope.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I went to the bank for get money.✓Right: I went to the bank to get money.

Addition, examples, sequence and summary

FunctionLinking words
additionand, also, too, as well, in addition (to), besides, moreover, furthermore, what's more
examplesfor example, for instance, such as, like, including
sequencefirst, second, then, next, after that, finally, lastly
summaryin conclusion, to sum up, in short, overall
conditionif, unless, provided that, in case (see conditionals)

As prepositions, in addition to, besides and apart from are followed by a noun or -ing: "In addition to working full-time, he studies at night." Besides can also start a sentence: "Besides, it's too late."

Time clauses

when, while, as, before, after, until, as soon as, once, by the time, whenever. In a time clause about the future, use a present tense, not will: "I'll call you as soon as I arrive."

  • While I was waiting, I read the manual.
  • As soon as the alarm rang, everyone ran outside.
  • By the time we got there, the meeting had ended.

Negative words first: inversion

When a negative or limiting word starts the sentence for emphasis, the auxiliary comes before the subject, as in a question. This is formal English.

NormalInverted
I have never seen such a mess.Never have I seen such a mess.
He not only speaks French, but he also writes it.Not only does he speak French, but he also writes it.
We had hardly arrived when it started to rain.Hardly had we arrived when it started to rain.
He had no sooner left than the phone rang.No sooner had he left than the phone rang.
You will understand only then.Only then will you understand.
We rarely see such courage.Rarely do we see such courage.
You must not open this door under any circumstances.Under no circumstances must you open this door.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: Never I have seen such a mess.✓Right: Never have I seen such a mess.
