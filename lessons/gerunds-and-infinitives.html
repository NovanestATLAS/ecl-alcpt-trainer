Gerunds and infinitives: -ing or to + verb?

When one verb follows another, the second verb takes one of three forms: the gerund (doing), the to-infinitive (to do) or the base form (do). The first word decides which one.

Which verb form after which word?

After…UseExample
a modal (can, must, will, should…) and do / does / didbase formYou must go. · Did you see it?
make, let, had better, would ratherbase formThey let us leave.
to (part of the infinitive)base formI want to leave.
a preposition (in, on, at, of, for, about, without, before, after)-ingShe is good at drawing. · He left without saying goodbye.
be (progressive)-ingThey are working.
be (passive)past participleThe door was opened.
have (perfect)past participleWe have finished.

ECL tipWatch out for to as a preposition: look forward to, be used to, get used to, object to, be committed to take -ing: "I look forward to seeing you."

Verbs + gerund (-ing)

These verbs are followed by -ing: admit, avoid, consider, delay, deny, dislike, enjoy, finish, give up, imagine, involve, keep, mention, mind, miss, postpone, practice, quit, recommend, risk, suggest, can't help, can't stand, it's no use, it's worth.

  • He avoided answering the question.
  • Would you mind waiting a moment?
  • I can't help laughing when he tells that story.
  • This museum is worth visiting.

A gerund can also be the subject of a sentence: "Running is good for your health."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I enjoy to swim.✓Right: I enjoy swimming.

Verbs + to-infinitive

These verbs are followed by to + verb: afford, agree, appear, arrange, ask, attempt, choose, decide, deserve, expect, fail, hope, learn, manage, need, offer, plan, prepare, pretend, promise, refuse, seem, tend, threaten, wait, want, would like.

  • We decided to postpone the trip.
  • She refused to sign the paper.
  • I can't afford to buy a new car.

Verb + person + to-infinitive: advise, allow, ask, encourage, expect, force, invite, order, persuade, remind, teach, tell, want, warn.

  • The officer ordered the men to stop.
  • I want you to listen carefully.

The to-infinitive also shows purpose: "He went to the PX to buy batteries."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I want that you listen.✓Right: I want you to listen.

Verbs that take both, with the same meaning

begin, start, continue, like, love, hate, prefer can take either form with little change in meaning: "It started raining." = "It started to rain."

Verbs that change meaning

Verb+ -ing+ to-infinitive
stopstop an activity: He stopped smoking. (He doesn't smoke now.)stop in order to do something: He stopped to smoke. (He stopped walking and smoked.)
rememberremember something you did: I remember locking the door.remember a duty: Remember to lock the door.
forgetforget something you did: I'll never forget seeing the Grand Canyon.forget a duty: Don't forget to call me.
trydo it as an experiment: Try restarting the computer.make an effort: I tried to lift the box, but it was too heavy.
regretbe sorry about the past: I regret saying that.be sorry to announce: We regret to inform you that the flight is canceled.
meaninvolve: The new job means traveling a lot.intend: I meant to call you, but I forgot.

Used to, be used to, get used to

StructureMeaningExample
used to + base verba past habit or state that has stoppedI used to smoke. (I don't now.)
be used to + -ing or a nounbe accustomed toI'm used to getting up early.
get used to + -ing or a nounbecome accustomed toYou'll soon get used to the cold.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I am used to get up early.✓Right: I am used to getting up early.

How the ECL and ALCPT test gerunds and infinitives

The test gives a verb, adjective or preposition and asks which form comes next. Look at the word just before the blank: after a preposition you always need -ing; after many verbs you need to + verb; after others, -ing; and a few verbs change meaning.

Practice itemAnswerWhy
The sergeant suggested ___ early.leavingSuggest is followed by -ing, like avoid, enjoy, finish, keep, mind.
I'm looking forward to ___ my family.seeingHere to is a preposition (look forward to something), so -ing follows.
The driver stopped ___ the map.to checkStop + to-infinitive means stop in order to do something. Stopped checking would mean he no longer checked it.
She is used to ___ up early.gettingBe used to means be accustomed to, and takes -ing. Compare She used to get up late (a past habit).

Other expressions where to is a preposition: be used to, get used to, object to, be committed to, when it comes to. All take -ing.

Tricky cases

These patterns are not about which verb comes first, so learners often miss them.

  • An -ing form can be the subject: Swimming is good exercise.
  • Adjective + to-infinitive: It is easy to learn. The box is too heavy to carry.
  • Verb + person + to-infinitive: The captain ordered us to wait. She asked me to call her. The same with want, tell, advise, allow.
  • Make and let take the base form, without to: The instructor made us run. Let me help.
  • Need + -ing has a passive meaning: The truck needs washing = it needs to be washed.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I enjoy to read.✓Right: I enjoy reading.
  • ✗Wrong: He let me to go home early.✓Right: He let me go home early.
  • ✗Wrong: I am interested to learn Spanish.✓Right: I am interested in learning Spanish.
