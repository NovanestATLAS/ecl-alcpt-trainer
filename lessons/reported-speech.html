Reported speech: statements, questions and commands

Direct speech repeats the exact words: He said, "I am tired." Reported (indirect) speech gives the meaning, without quotation marks: He said (that) he was tired. When the reporting verb is in the past, the tenses usually move one step back.

Tense changes (backshift)

Direct speechReported speech
present simple: "I work here."past simple: He said he worked there.
present progressive: "I am working."past progressive: He said he was working.
present perfect: "I have finished."past perfect: He said he had finished.
past simple: "I finished at six."past perfect: He said he had finished at six (or finished).
will: "I will call you."would: He said he would call me.
can: "I can swim."could: He said he could swim.
may: "I may be late."might: He said he might be late.
must: "You must go."had to (or must): He said I had to go.

The past perfect and would, could, might, should, ought to do not change.

ECL tipIf the reporting verb is in the present (says, has said), or if the statement is still true, the tense does not need to change: "She says she is tired." · "He said the Earth is round."

Say or tell?

VerbPatternExample
saysay (that)… · say something to someoneHe said (that) he was ready. · He said goodbye to us.
telltell + person (that)…He told me (that) he was ready.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He said me that he was ready.✓Right: He told me that he was ready.
  • ✗Wrong: He told that he was ready.✓Right: He said that he was ready.

Reported questions

A reported question has the word order of a statement (subject + verb), no do / does / did, and no question mark.

Yes/no questions

ask + if / whether + subject + verb

Use if or whether when there is no question word.

  • "Are you ready?" → She asked if I was ready.
  • "Did you see the accident?" → The officer asked whether I had seen the accident.

Wh- questions

ask + question word + subject + verb

Keep the question word: who, what, where, when, why, how…

  • "Where do you live?" → He asked me where I lived.
  • "What time will the convoy leave?" → She asked what time the convoy would leave.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He asked me where did I live.✓Right: He asked me where I lived.
  • ✗Wrong: She asked me if I was hungry?✓Right: She asked me if I was hungry.

Commands, requests and advice

Commands and requests

tell / ask / order / advise + person + (not) to + verb

The imperative becomes a to-infinitive.

  • "Close the door." → He told me to close the door.
  • "Don't move!" → The sergeant ordered us not to move.
  • "Please help me." → She asked me to help her.
  • "You should rest." → The doctor advised me to rest.

Reporting verbs

Reporting verbs give the meaning of what was said. Each one has its own pattern.

PatternVerbsExample
verb + to-infinitiveagree, offer, promise, refuse, threatenHe offered to drive us to the airport.
verb + person + to-infinitiveadvise, ask, order, remind, tell, warn, encourageShe reminded me to lock the door.
verb + -ingadmit, deny, recommend, suggestHe denied taking the money.
verb + preposition + -ingapologize for, insist on, accuse someone of, blame someone forHe apologized for being late.
verb + that…explain, complain, announce, claim, admitShe explained that the road was closed.
suggest, recommend + that + subject + base verb(the subjunctive)The doctor suggested that he rest.

Suggest never takes a person + to-infinitive. Say "He suggested going" or "He suggested that we go", not He suggested us to go. More in the lesson on the subjunctive.
