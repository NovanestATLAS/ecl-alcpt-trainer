English verb tenses: the 12 tenses with examples

A tense tells you when an action happens and how it relates to another time. For each tense, learn the form, the use and the signal words: on the ECL, a time word such as since, ago or by the time is often the fastest way to the right answer.

How the tenses are built

Every tense combines a time (present, past or future) with an aspect: simple, progressive (also called continuous), perfect or perfect progressive.

AspectFormWhat it shows
Simplebase verb, verb + -s, V2, will + verba fact, a habit or a completed action
Progressivebe + verb-ingan action in progress at a certain time
Perfecthave + past participle (V3)an action or situation before, or up to, a certain time
Perfect progressivehave been + verb-ingan action that continued up to a certain time, often with its duration

V1 = base form (go), V2 = simple past (went), V3 = past participle (gone). Irregular verbs must be learned by heart: see the irregular verbs list.

Present tenses

Present simple

I/you/we/they work · he/she/it works · do/does not work

Habits and routines, facts, timetables, and state verbs (know, like, want, own, need, believe).

  • The platoon trains every morning at 0600.
  • Water freezes at 0 °C.
  • The bus to the base leaves at 7:15 tomorrow.

Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, on Mondays, once a week.

Present progressive

am / is / are + verb-ing

An action happening now or around now, a temporary situation, or a fixed arrangement in the future.

  • Be quiet. The captain is speaking.
  • I am staying in the barracks this week.
  • We are meeting the new instructor on Friday.

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, currently, these days, Look! Listen!

Present perfect

have / has + V3

A past action with a result now, an experience at an unknown time, or a situation that started in the past and continues now.

  • I have lost my ID card. (I don't have it now.)
  • She has never been to Texas.
  • He has served in the Navy since 2015.

Signal words: since, for, already, yet, just, ever, never, so far, recently, up to now, this week.

Present perfect progressive

have / has been + verb-ing

An action that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped, with the focus on how long.

  • We have been waiting for the helicopter for two hours.
  • Your eyes are red. Have you been reading all night?

Signal words: for, since, how long, all day, lately.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She works here since 2020.✓Right: She has worked here since 2020.
  • ✗Wrong: I am knowing the answer.✓Right: I know the answer.

Past tenses

Past simple

V2 (worked, went) · did not + verb

A finished action at a finished time.

  • The exercise ended yesterday.
  • They flew to Germany two years ago.

Signal words: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when I was a child.

Past progressive

was / were + verb-ing

An action in progress at a moment in the past, often interrupted by a shorter action in the past simple.

  • I was cleaning my rifle when the alarm went off.
  • At 9 p.m. last night we were driving back to the base.

Signal words: while, when, as, at that moment, at 5 o'clock yesterday.

Past perfect

had + V3

The earlier of two past actions.

  • When we arrived, the convoy had already left.
  • He had never seen snow before he moved to Alaska.

Signal words: before, after, by the time, already, when + past simple.

Past perfect progressive

had been + verb-ing

An action that continued up to a moment in the past.

  • The troops were tired because they had been marching since dawn.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I have seen him yesterday.✓Right: I saw him yesterday.

Future tenses

Future simple

will + verb

Predictions, promises, offers, and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

  • I think it will rain tonight.
  • Wait, I'll help you with that box.

Future progressive

will be + verb-ing

An action in progress at a moment in the future.

  • This time tomorrow we will be flying over the Atlantic.

Future perfect

will have + V3

An action completed before a moment in the future.

  • By Friday, the engineers will have repaired the bridge.

Signal words: by, by the time, by then, before.

Future perfect progressive

will have been + verb-ing

How long an action will have continued at a moment in the future.

  • In June, she will have been working here for ten years.

Other ways to talk about the future

FormUseExample
be going to + verba plan or intention decided before nowWe're going to paint the barracks next week.
be going to + verba prediction based on what you can see nowLook at those clouds. It's going to rain.
present progressivea fixed arrangement, with a time and a placeI'm seeing the doctor at 10 tomorrow.
present simpletimetables and schedulesThe flight departs at 0830.
be about to + verbsomething that will happen very soonHurry up, the bus is about to leave.
be to + verbofficial plans and ordersThe general is to visit the base on Monday.

ECL tipAfter when, before, after, as soon as, until, if and unless, use a present tense for the future: "Call me when you arrive" (not when you will arrive).

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: When he will come, I will tell him.✓Right: When he comes, I will tell him.

Signal words at a glance

You see…Think of…
since, for, yet, already, ever, justpresent perfect (simple or progressive)
yesterday, ago, last…, in 2010past simple
while, at that moment (in the past)past progressive
by the time, before (two past actions)past perfect
by + a future timefuture perfect
now, at the moment, Look!present progressive
every…, usually, alwayspresent simple

In American English, just, already and yet also go with the past simple: "Did you eat yet?" And for + a finished period takes the past simple: "He served in the Army for ten years" (he is not in the Army now).
