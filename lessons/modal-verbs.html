Modal verbs: can, could, may, might, must, have to, should

Modal verbs (can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, ought to) add a meaning to the main verb: ability, permission, obligation, advice, or how sure you are. They all follow the same three rules.

Three rules of modal verbs

  • A modal is followed by the base form, without to: "You must go" (not must to go, not must goes). The exception is ought to.
  • A modal never takes -s: "She can swim" (not she cans).
  • Questions and negatives need no do: "Can you help?" · "You shouldn't smoke here."

Have to and need to are not true modals: they change with the subject ("he has to") and use do in questions and negatives ("Do you have to go?").

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He can speaks three languages.✓Right: He can speak three languages.
  • ✗Wrong: You must to wear a helmet.✓Right: You must wear a helmet.

Ability

can / could / be able to

can (present) · could (past) · be able to (every tense)

What someone is able to do.

  • She can speak four languages.
  • When I was young, I could run ten kilometers easily.
  • After the course, you will be able to repair this radio.

For one specific success in the past, use was able to or managed to, not could: "The pilot was able to land safely."

Permission and requests

can / could / may

can (informal) · could (polite) · may (formal)

Asking for and giving permission, and making requests.

  • Can I use your phone?
  • Could you open the window, please?
  • May I come in, sir?
  • You may leave when you have finished the test.

Would you mind + -ing is a very polite request: "Would you mind closing the door?"

Obligation, no obligation and prohibition

ModalMeaningExample
muststrong obligation, often from the speaker or a ruleYou must wear your ID at all times.
have toobligation from outside: rules, the situationWe have to report at 0600.
need toit is necessaryI need to renew my passport.
must not (mustn't)it is forbiddenYou must not smoke near the fuel truck.
don't have to, don't need to (British also: needn't)it is not necessary: you can chooseYou don't have to wear a uniform on Sunday.
had toobligation in the past (must has no past form)We had to walk because the truck broke down.

ECL tipMust not and don't have to are very different. "You must not tell him" means it is forbidden. "You don't have to tell him" means it isn't necessary.

Advice

should / ought to / had better

should, ought to: advice · had better: strong advice, with a warning

What is a good idea, or the right thing to do.

  • You should see a doctor about that cough.
  • You ought to check the tires before a long trip.
  • You'd better leave now, or you'll miss the bus.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: You had better to hurry.✓Right: You had better hurry.

Possibility and deduction

These modals show how sure you are about something now.

How sure?ModalExample
almost 100% sure it is truemustThe lights are on. They must be at home.
possiblemay, might, couldHe may be in the office. · It might rain later.
almost 100% sure it is not truecan't, couldn'tThat can't be John. He's in Germany this week.
almost sure it is not true (American)must notHe must not be home. His car is gone.

Past modals: modal + have + past participle

FormMeaningExample
should have + V3it was a good idea, but you didn't do it (criticism, regret)You should have called me.
shouldn't have + V3you did it, and it was a mistakeI shouldn't have eaten so much.
must have + V3I am sure it happenedThe ground is wet. It must have rained.
can't have, couldn't have + V3I am sure it didn't happenHe can't have seen us. It was too dark.
may have, might have + V3perhaps it happenedShe might have missed the bus.
could have + V3it was possible, but it didn't happen; or perhaps it happenedYou could have been hurt! · He could have missed the bus.
didn't need to + verb (British also: needn't have + V3)it was not necessary (often: you did it anyway)We didn't need to hurry. The meeting was postponed.
would have + V3an imaginary past (third conditional)I would have helped you if I had known.
