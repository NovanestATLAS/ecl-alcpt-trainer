Adjectives, adverbs and comparisons

Adjectives describe nouns (a fast car). Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives or other adverbs (he drives fast, very quickly). Comparisons show how two or more things are different or the same.

Adjectives and adverbs

AdjectiveAdverbExample
quickquicklyHe is a quick runner. · He runs quickly.
carefulcarefullyDrive carefully.
goodwellShe's a good driver. · She drives well.
fast, hard, late, earlyfast, hard, late, early (same form)He works hard. (hardly means almost not)

After linking verbs (be, seem, look, feel, sound, taste, smell, become, get), use an adjective: "You look tired." · "This soup tastes good."

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He speaks English very good.✓Right: He speaks English very well.

-ed and -ing adjectives

-ing: what causes the feeling-ed: how the person feels
The lecture was boring.We were bored.
The news is surprising.I was surprised.
The movie was frightening.The children were frightened.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: I am very interesting in history.✓Right: I am very interested in history.

Comparatives and superlatives

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
one syllable: talltaller (than)the tallest
ends in -e: largelargerthe largest
one vowel + one consonant: bigbiggerthe biggest
two syllables ending in -y: easyeasierthe easiest
most adjectives of two or more syllables: difficultmore difficultthe most difficult
irregular: good, badbetter, worsethe best, the worst
irregular: farfarther, furtherthe farthest, the furthest
irregular: much / many, littlemore, lessthe most, the least

A few two-syllable adjectives can also take -er: quieter, simpler, narrower.

  • The new rifle is lighter than the old one.
  • This is the most difficult exercise of the course.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: She is more taller than me.✓Right: She is taller than me.
  • ✗Wrong: It's the most cheapest one.✓Right: It's the cheapest one.

As… as, and other comparisons

StructureExample
as + adjective or adverb + as (equal)He is as tall as his father.
not as / not so… as (less)The test was not as hard as I expected.
the same + noun + asShe is the same age as me.
twice / three times as… asThis truck is twice as heavy as that one.
the + comparative…, the + comparativeThe more you practice, the better you get.
comparative + and + comparativeIt's getting colder and colder.
different from · similar toMy answer is different from yours.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He is as tall than me.✓Right: He is as tall as me.

Too, enough, so and such

StructureMeaningExample
too + adjective (+ to + verb)more than is rightHe is too young to drive.
adjective + enough (+ to + verb)sufficientHe is old enough to drive.
enough + nouna sufficient quantityWe don't have enough water.
so + adjective or adverb + thata resultIt was so cold that the engine froze.
such (a/an) + adjective + noun + thata resultIt was such a cold night that the engine froze.

Common mistakes

  • ✗Wrong: He is enough old.✓Right: He is old enough.

The order of adjectives

When several adjectives come before a noun, the usual order is: opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material, purpose, then the noun.

  • a nice big old house · a small black leather bag · a new American fighter jet
