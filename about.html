About this site

A free place to practice the English that the ECL and the ALCPT test, made by an independent studio. Here is what the site offers, how the questions are written and checked, and who is behind it.

Updated October 18, 2026

ECL & ALCPT English Trainer is a free place to practice the English that the English Comprehension Level test (ECL) and the American Language Course Placement Test (ALCPT) measure: listening and reading in American English. It started as an Android app for people preparing for the ECL. This website gives the same practice to anyone with a browser, on a computer or a phone, without an account.

What you will find here

  • The trainer: 4,363 multiple-choice questions in grammar, vocabulary, idioms and phrasal verbs, each with a short explanation; 5,041 listening questions (short statements, questions and answers, dialogs, conversations and short talks); 80 numbered ALCPT/ECL practice tests of 50 listening and 50 reading questions; timed tests; and a mistake box that brings back the questions you missed.
  • Grammar lessons that explain each rule with examples and quick checks, plus reference pages: 309 phrasal verbs, ECL vocabulary by theme, American idioms and irregular verbs.
  • Practice pages by topic: the rules in brief, the most common mistakes and 15 questions with full explanations on one grammar point.
  • Free tests with answers that you can take in a few minutes, and guides on the tests, study plans, listening, vocabulary and test-taking strategies.

Who makes it

The site and the app are published by Novanest ATLAS, an independent app studio. They are paid for by advertising, so they stay free to use. Advertising never decides what we write: no page is written for an advertiser, and ads are kept apart from the questions and lessons.

How the content is written

All 4,363 questions and all the lessons are original. They are not copied from any real ECL or ALCPT form, which are controlled documents. They are written to train the same skills: choosing a verb form, a preposition or a word in context, and understanding spoken American English.

Each question has four choices and one correct answer. The explanation says why the answer is right and, where it helps, why a tempting choice is wrong. Sentences use American spelling and vocabulary, and situations that learners meet at work, in training, when they travel and at home. About one question in five is easier, so that beginners can make progress too.

The listening questions are scripted as natural American speech and recorded with high-quality computer voices, a man's and a woman's, so that every one of the 5,041 questions can be heard. You can replay them, slow them down and read the text after you answer.

How we check it

New questions go through automatic checks before they are published: one right answer, four different choices, no repeated sentences, and balanced choices so that the right answer does not stand out by its length. Each one is reviewed again before it goes online. Older questions are reviewed regularly, and questions that learners report as unclear are rewritten or removed. Guides and practice pages show the date of their last update.

Practice percentages on this site are not ECL or ALCPT scores. They show how well you know the material we cover. To understand the real tests, read the ECL test explained and ECL scores and levels.

Found a mistake?

If an answer looks wrong or an explanation is unclear, tell us: copy the question and say which answer you think is right. We read every message and correct the question when you are right.

How to use the site

  1. Take the free ALCPT practice test to see where you stand.
  2. Pick your weakest area and read its lesson, then do the matching practice page.
  3. Practice a little every day in the trainer, and start each session with your mistake box.
  4. Once a week, take one of the 80 timed practice tests to measure your progress. The four-week study plan shows how to organize the weeks.

The Android app

The free Android app has the same questions, works without internet and can read each question and its choices aloud.

Privacy

You do not need an account. Your progress and your mistake box are saved in your own browser. The site uses Google AdSense for ads and Google Analytics to count visits; the privacy policy explains what this means and how to change your choices.
