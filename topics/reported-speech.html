Reported speech practice: 15 questions with explanations

How tenses move back and when they stay, how time words, questions and orders change, and which pattern each reporting verb takes. Short rules first, then 15 questions with full explanations.

Updated October 18, 2026

Reported speech is how you pass on what someone said without quoting the exact words. On Monday your supervisor says, "I'll sign it tomorrow." On Friday you tell a coworker: She said she would sign it the next day. Four things changed: the pronoun, the verb, the time word and the quotation marks.

That is a lot to track at once, which is why reported speech costs points in multiple-choice grammar questions. One reported question left in question word order, one said where English needs told, or one to after a verb that wants -ing, and the answer is wrong. In listening, people report other people's words all the time: My boss asked me to stay late. You need to hear at once whether it was an order, a question, an offer or a warning.

This page gives you the rules in short tables, the mistakes learners make most, and 15 new practice questions. Each explanation shows why the right answer works and why the most tempting wrong answer does not. For the full lesson, read Reported speech.

Reported speech in brief

Backshift: one step back

After a past reporting verb (said, told, asked), the verb usually moves one step into the past. If you need to review the tenses, see Verb tenses.

Direct speechReported speech
"I work nights."He said he worked nights.
"I am packing."She said she was packing.
"We have finished."They said they had finished.
"I lost my badge."He said he had lost (or lost) his badge.
"I will call you."She said she would call me.
"I can help."He said he could help.
"It may rain."She said it might rain.
"You must rest."The nurse said I had to rest.

Would, could, should, might and had + V3 stay the same.

When the tense does not change

  • The reporting verb is in the present: The pharmacy says your pills are ready.
  • What was said is still true: She told me she has two sons. (Had is also correct.)

Pronouns, time and place words

Pronouns change to fit the new speaker (our becomes their). When the report comes later or from another place, these words change too:

DirectReported
today, tonightthat day, that night
yesterdaythe day before, the previous day
tomorrowthe next day, the following day
next week, last weekthe following week, the week before
two days agotwo days before, two days earlier
now, here, thisthen, there, that

Say or tell?

Tell needs a person: He told me the store was closed. Say has no person, or uses to: He said (to me) that the store was closed. So never said me, and never told that with no person.

Questions

Use statement word order, no do, does or did, and no question mark. Yes/no questions take if or whether: "Do you need a ride?" → He asked if I needed a ride. Wh- questions keep the question word: "Where did you park?" → She asked where I had parked. More on word order in Questions and tags.

Commands and requests

tell, ask, remind + person + (not) to + verb: "Don't touch the wet paint." → The painter told us not to touch the wet paint.

Reporting verbs

PatternVerbsExample
verb + to + verboffer, promise, agree, refuseShe offered to lend me her car.
verb + person + to + verbwarn, advise, remind, inviteThe guide warned us not to feed the bears.
verb + -ingdeny, admit, suggest, recommendHe admitted breaking the printer.
verb + preposition + -ingapologize for, insist onShe apologized for missing the meeting.
verb + that + clauseexplain, complain, suggestHe explained that the bus was late.

Suggest never takes a person + to: say He suggested leaving early or He suggested that we leave early. More verbs like these are in Gerunds and infinitives.

Common mistakes

  • Not: My boss said me to finish the report. Say: My boss told me to finish the report. Say never takes a person right after it, and orders are reported with tell + person + to.
  • Not: The nurse asked me what was my name. Say: The nurse asked me what my name was. In a reported question, the subject (my name) comes before the verb.
  • Not: The clerk asked me do I have a receipt. Say: The clerk asked me if I had a receipt. A yes/no question needs if or whether, statement word order and no do.
  • Not: She said she will call me, but she never did. Say: She said she would call me, but she never did. After a past reporting verb, will becomes would, especially when the moment has passed.
  • Not: The doctor told me don't lift anything heavy. Say: The doctor told me not to lift anything heavy. A negative order becomes not to + verb.
  • Not: She suggested me to call a plumber. Say: She suggested calling a plumber. Suggest is followed by -ing or a that clause (suggested that I call), never by a person + to.
  • Not: The driver denied to run the red light. Say: The driver denied running the red light. Deny, like admit, is followed by -ing.

Practice questions

Choose an answer for each sentence, then open Show the answer to check it and read why the other choices are wrong.

  1. The mechanic ______ the brakes were fine, but the tires needed replacing soon.

    • Atold
    • Bsaid
    • Ctold to
    • Dsaid me
    Show the answer

    B. said

    Tell must have a person right after it, and say must not. No person follows the blank here, so said is right: He said (that) the brakes were fine. Told is the trap; it would need a person, as in He told me the brakes were fine. Said me is never correct, and told to does not fit here.

  2. "I'm working from home today," my sister said. → My sister said she ______ from home that day.

    • Awas working
    • Bis working
    • Chas worked
    • Dworks
    Show the answer

    A. was working

    After a past reporting verb, the present progressive (am working) moves back to the past progressive: was working. The words that day show the report comes later, so is working is the trap: it keeps the original tense, but your sister's today is over. Has worked and works change the meaning.

  3. "I'll fix your bike tomorrow," Tom told me last Friday. → Last Friday, Tom told me he ______.

    • Awill fix my bike tomorrow
    • Bwould fix your bike the next day
    • Cwould fix my bike tomorrow
    • Dwould fix my bike the next day
    Show the answer

    D. would fix my bike the next day

    Three things change. Will becomes would after the past verb told. Tomorrow becomes the next day, because Tom meant the day after last Friday, not the day after today. Your becomes my, because the owner of the bike is now the one speaking. Would fix my bike tomorrow is the trap: it changes the verb but keeps tomorrow.

  4. "Is the pharmacy open on Sundays?" I asked the nurse. → I asked the nurse ______ open on Sundays.

    • Athat the pharmacy was
    • Bwhether the pharmacy was
    • Cwas the pharmacy
    • Dwhether was the pharmacy
    Show the answer

    B. whether the pharmacy was

    A yes/no question is reported with if or whether, followed by normal statement order, subject then verb: whether the pharmacy was. Was the pharmacy is the trap, because it keeps the question word order, and reported questions never do. That is for statements, not questions, and whether was the pharmacy mixes two mistakes.

  5. "I can drive you to the clinic if you like," Maria said. → Maria ______ to the clinic.

    • Aoffered me to drive
    • Boffered to drive me
    • Coffered driving me
    • Doffered me driving
    Show the answer

    B. offered to drive me

    Offer is followed directly by to + verb, and the person comes after that verb: offered to drive me. Offered me to drive is the trap. It copies told me to and asked me to, but offer, promise, agree and refuse never put a person before to. You can offer someone a thing (She offered me a ride), but not offer someone to do something.

  6. Dan said he ______ his car keys, but he found them in his jacket pocket ten minutes later.

    • Ahas lost
    • Bwas losing
    • Chad lost
    • Dwould lose
    Show the answer

    C. had lost

    Dan's words were "I've lost my car keys." After a past reporting verb, the present perfect moves back to the past perfect: had lost. Has lost is the trap. You can keep the original tense only when something is still true, and here it is not, because he found the keys ten minutes later. Would lose is a future form.

  7. "Keep your back straight," the trainer said to me. → The trainer told me ______ my back straight.

    • Ato keep
    • Bkeep
    • Ckeeping
    • Dthat I keep
    Show the answer

    A. to keep

    An order or instruction is reported with tell + person + to + verb: told me to keep. Keep without to is the trap. It copies the imperative from the quotation, but after told me, English needs the to-infinitive. Keeping and that I keep are not used after tell when you report an instruction.

  8. Good news: the airline says our flight ______ at 6:15 now, not 5:40, so we have time for breakfast.

    • Aleft
    • Bwould leave
    • Chad left
    • Dleaves
    Show the answer

    D. leaves

    The reporting verb says is in the present, so there is no backshift: leaves, the present simple we use for timetables. Would leave is the trap. Many learners move every reported verb back, but backshift only happens after a past reporting verb like said or told. Left and had left would put the flight in the past.

  1. "Why don't we take the train instead of driving?" my wife said. → My wife ______ the train instead of driving.

    • Asuggested us to take
    • Bsuggested to take
    • Csuggested taking
    • Dsuggested we taking
    Show the answer

    C. suggested taking

    Suggest is followed by -ing (suggested taking) or by a that clause with a base verb (suggested that we take). Suggested us to take is the trap. It looks like told us to take, but suggest never takes a person + to-infinitive. Suggested to take and suggested we taking are wrong too.

  2. "What time does the cafeteria close?" my new coworker asked me. → My new coworker asked me what time ______.

    • Adoes the cafeteria close
    • Bdid the cafeteria close
    • Cthe cafeteria closed
    • Dthe cafeteria did close
    Show the answer

    C. the cafeteria closed

    A reported wh- question keeps the question word (what time) and then uses statement order with no do, does or did: the cafeteria closed. Did the cafeteria close is the trap: it moves the tense back correctly, but it keeps the question form. The cafeteria did close is an emphatic statement, not a reported question.

  3. "If I were you, I wouldn't swim here. The current is very strong," the lifeguard said. → The lifeguard ______ us not to swim there.

    • Awarned
    • Bsaid
    • Csuggested
    • Ddenied
    Show the answer

    A. warned

    When someone tells you about a danger, report it with warn + person + not to + verb: warned us not to swim. Said is the trap: say can never have a person right after it (said us). Suggested does not take a person + to, and denied means saying you did not do something. Notice also that here becomes there.

  4. In March, my cousin wrote, "We're moving to Ohio next month." → In March, my cousin wrote that they were moving to Ohio ______.

    • Anext month
    • Bthe month before
    • Cthe following month
    • Dlast month
    Show the answer

    C. the following month

    Time words change when the report comes later. In a message written in March, next month means April, so the report says the following month (or the next month). Next month is the trap: said today, it would mean the month after now, which is a different month. The month before and last month point the wrong way.

  5. "No, I didn't use your laptop," my roommate said. → My roommate ______ using my laptop.

    • Arefused
    • Brejected
    • Cdisagreed
    • Ddenied
    Show the answer

    D. denied

    To deny something is to say you did not do it, and deny is followed by -ing: denied using. Refused is the trap: refuse means saying no to a request or an order, and it takes to + verb (refused to use). You reject an offer or an idea, and you disagree with an opinion.

  6. "Please don't park in front of our garage," our new neighbors said. → Our new neighbors asked us ______ in front of their garage.

    • Anot to park
    • Bto don't park
    • Cdon't park
    • Dnot park
    Show the answer

    A. not to park

    A negative request becomes ask + person + not to + verb, with not before to: asked us not to park. Don't park is the trap, because it copies the words in the quotation, but a reported request or command always uses the infinitive. Notice also that our garage becomes their garage, since the neighbors are not the speakers now.

  7. Sam told me he ______ to my party, but in the end he came and stayed until midnight.

    • Acan't come
    • Bcouldn't come
    • Ccouldn't have come
    • Dhasn't come
    Show the answer

    B. couldn't come

    Sam's words were "I can't come." After told, can moves back to could: he couldn't come. Can't come is the trap. You can keep can when a situation is still true now, but here it is over: the party happened, and Sam came after all. Couldn't have come is about a past possibility, which is not what Sam said.

Tips for the test

  • Look at the reporting verb first. After a past verb (said, told, asked), expect the tense to move back. After a present verb (says, tells), keep it.
  • Check the word right after the blank. A person (me, us, the driver) points to told, asked, warned, advised or reminded. No person points to said, explained or suggested.
  • Cross out question word order. In a reported question, any choice with did I, was he or do you is wrong.
  • Learn reporting verbs as chunks. Say them aloud with their pattern: offer to help, warn someone not to go, deny doing, suggest doing, apologize for doing, insist on doing.
  • Use the time clues. Words like that day, the next day or but in the end show that the report comes later, so the tense moves back.
  • Practice with sound. The free trainer has recorded conversations and short talks with Slower and Show the text buttons. For a listening set, try ECL listening practice.

Questions learners ask

Do I always have to change the tense in reported speech?

No. If the reporting verb is in the present (says, has told), keep the original tense. After a past reporting verb, moving the tense back is normal, but you may keep it when the fact is still true: She said she lives in Denver, if she still does. In a test question, look for clues such as that day or but in the end. They show the situation is over, so the tense must move back.

Can I leave out "that" after said and told?

Yes. In everyday English, that is often dropped: He said (that) he was tired. She told me (that) the store was closed. Both versions are correct. But never use that to report a question. Use if or whether for yes/no questions, and the question word for wh- questions: She asked if I was tired, not She asked that I was tired.

Is there any difference between if and whether?

In most reported questions they mean the same, and if is more common in speech: He asked if (or whether) I was coming. Use whether, not if, right before or not (whether or not), after a preposition (We talked about whether to stay) and before a to-infinitive (I can't decide whether to go).
