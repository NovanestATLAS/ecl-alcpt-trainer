Passive voice practice: 15 questions with explanations

Be + past participle in every tense, modal passives, when to keep by + agent, the get-passive and forms like is said to. Short rules first, then 15 questions with full explanations.

Updated October 18, 2026

In a passive sentence, the subject receives the action instead of doing it: The meeting has been moved to Friday. Nobody says who moved it, because it doesn't matter. That is why the passive is everywhere in the English you meet at work, on base and when you travel: notices, emails, safety rules, news and instructions.

It also costs points. In a multiple-choice grammar question, the four choices often look almost the same: was repaired, was repairing, has repaired, had been repaired. To choose well, you have to answer two questions fast. Does the subject do the action, or does it receive it? And which tense does the time in the sentence ask for? In listening, the small words been and being are short and easy to miss, but they change the meaning.

This page gives you the forms in one table, the modal and reporting patterns, the mistakes learners make most often, and 15 new practice questions. Each explanation tells you why the right answer works and why the most tempting wrong answer does not. If you want the full lesson first, read The passive voice.

The passive in brief

Passive = a form of be + the past participle (V3). The form of be shows the tense; the past participle never changes.

TenseFormExample
Present simpleam / is / are + V3Visitors are asked to sign in at the front desk.
Present progressiveam / is / are being + V3The gym floor is being cleaned, so use the side door.
Past simplewas / were + V3Our bags were checked twice at the airport.
Past progressivewas / were being + V3Last week my car was being repaired, so I took the bus.
Present perfecthas / have been + V3My bank card has been blocked, so I can't pay online.
Past perfecthad been + V3When we arrived, the gate had already been closed.
Futurewill be + V3The new schedule will be posted on Monday.
Future perfectwill have been + V3By noon, all the boxes will have been delivered.
be going toam / is / are going to be + V3The old clinic is going to be replaced.

Only verbs that take an object can be passive. Happen, arrive, die, occur and belong have no passive: The delay happened at noon, not was happened.

Passive with modals

  • Now or later: modal + be + V3. Your ID must be checked at the door.
  • Past: modal + have been + V3. The gas bill should have been paid last week. (It wasn't.)

When to keep by + agent

Keep by + the doer only when it is new or important information: The new dining hall was designed by a local architect. Leave it out when the doer is unknown, obvious or not important: My bike was stolen. For a tool, use with: The tape was cut with scissors.

The get-passive

In everyday speech, get + V3 often replaces be for things that happen to people, often by accident or as a surprise: He got hurt playing soccer. Our flight got canceled. In formal writing, use be.

Reporting passives

With say, believe, think, know, report and expect, formal English has two patterns: It is known that the old bridge needs repairs. = The old bridge is known to need repairs. For an earlier action, use to have + V3: The hikers are believed to have spent the night in a cabin.

Related: have or get something done

When someone does a job for you, use have or get + object + V3: I had my eyes tested. We're getting the car serviced on Friday. The object comes before the participle. The causative lesson has more.

Common mistakes

  • Not: The meeting canceled because of the snow. Say: The meeting was canceled because of the snow. Without was, the sentence says the meeting canceled something.
  • Not: The trucks are wash every Friday. Say: The trucks are washed every Friday. After be you need the past participle, and its -ed ending must be there even when it is hard to hear.
  • Not: The package was arrived this morning. Say: The package arrived this morning. Arrive has no object, so it has no passive.
  • Not: The road is repaired this week, so take the other route. Say: The road is being repaired this week, so take the other route. For work in progress now, you need being.
  • Not: The report should be sent yesterday. Say: The report should have been sent yesterday. For the past, use modal + have been + V3.
  • Not: He is said that he speaks five languages. Say: He is said to speak five languages. Use It is said that he speaks or He is said to speak, never a mix of the two.
  • Not: I had cut my hair yesterday. Say: I had my hair cut yesterday. Had cut my hair means you cut it yourself; for a service, put the object before the participle.

Practice questions

Choose an answer for each sentence, then open Show the answer to check it and read why the other choices are wrong.

  1. In our office, every contract ______ by two managers before it goes out.

    • Ais signed
    • Bsigns
    • Cis signing
    • Dhas signed
    Show the answer

    A. is signed

    The contract does not sign anything; two managers sign it. So we need the passive, be + past participle, in the present simple for a regular routine: is signed. Signs, is signing and has signed are all active, so they would mean the contract itself does the signing.

  2. You'll have to take the stairs this week, because the elevator ______.

    • Ais repairing
    • Bis repaired
    • Chas repaired
    • Dis being repaired
    Show the answer

    D. is being repaired

    The work is in progress now and goes on all week, so we need the present progressive passive: is being + past participle. Is repaired is the trap. It is the simple form for routines, or it describes a finished state (the elevator works again), which does not fit taking the stairs. Is repairing is active.

  3. All the photos at my sister's wedding ______ by her best friend, who is a professional photographer.

    • Atook
    • Bhave taken
    • Cwere taken
    • Dwere taking
    Show the answer

    C. were taken

    The photos did not take anything; the friend took them. A past event with a plural subject needs were + past participle: were taken. Here by + agent is worth keeping, because who took the photos is the interesting part. Were taking is the trap: it is active and says the photos were taking something.

  4. Good news: your leave request ______, so you can book your flights now.

    • Ahas approved
    • Bis approving
    • Chas been approved
    • Dhad been approved
    Show the answer

    C. has been approved

    The result matters now (you can book your flights now), so we use the present perfect passive: has been + past participle. Has approved is the trap. It is active, so it would mean the request approved something. Had been approved is past perfect, which needs an earlier past moment to look back from, and there is none here.

  5. We had to wait in the hotel lobby for an hour because our room ______ when we got back from the beach.

    • Awas being cleaned
    • Bwas cleaning
    • Chad cleaned
    • Dhas been cleaned
    Show the answer

    A. was being cleaned

    When we got back, the cleaning was still going on, so we need the past progressive passive: was being + past participle. Was cleaning is the trap: it is active, so it says the room was cleaning something. Has been cleaned is present perfect, which does not fit a story told in the past.

  6. This medicine ______ in the refrigerator, or it will stop working.

    • Amust be stored
    • Bmust store
    • Cmust be storing
    • Dmust have stored
    Show the answer

    A. must be stored

    You store the medicine; the medicine does not store anything. A modal passive is modal + be + past participle: must be stored. Must store is the trap. It is active and needs an object after it, as in You must store it in the refrigerator. Must have stored is active too, and it talks about the past.

  7. By the time I called the restaurant, every table for Saturday night ______.

    • Ahas been booked
    • Bhad been booked
    • Chad booked
    • Dwas booking
    Show the answer

    B. had been booked

    By the time I called is a moment in the past, and the booking happened before it, so we need the past perfect passive: had been + past participle. Has been booked is the trap: the present perfect connects to now, not to a past moment. Had booked is active, as if the tables had booked something.

  8. My little brother ______ stung by a bee at the picnic, so we went home early.

    • Abecame
    • Bhad
    • Cmade
    • Dgot
    Show the answer

    D. got

    In everyday English, get + past participle is a passive for things that happen to people, often by accident: got stung, got hurt, got lost. Became is the trap, because get often means become, but become cannot form a passive. Had stung and made stung are not correct passive forms.

  1. The names of the winners ______ at the end of the evening, so please stay until nine.

    • Awill announce
    • Bwill be announced
    • Care announcing
    • Dwill have announced
    Show the answer

    B. will be announced

    The names do not announce anything; someone will announce them later tonight. That is the future passive: will be + past participle. Will announce is the trap. It is active, so the subject would have to be a person, as in The host will announce the winners. Are announcing and will have announced are active too.

  2. The new principal at my kids' school ______ very strict about homework.

    • Ais said to be
    • Bis said being
    • Csays to be
    • Dis said that she is
    Show the answer

    A. is said to be

    With say, believe and similar verbs, one formal pattern is subject + is said + to-infinitive: is said to be. The trap is is said that she is, which mixes two patterns. You can say It is said that she is strict or She is said to be strict, but not a mix. Is said being and says to be are not correct.

  3. The missing laptop ______ to security right away, not two days later.

    • Ashould report
    • Bshould have reported
    • Cshould have been reported
    • Dshould be reported
    Show the answer

    C. should have been reported

    The laptop went missing in the past, and nobody told security in time. For a past mistake with a passive meaning, use modal + have been + past participle: should have been reported. Should have reported is the trap: it is active and needs a person as the subject (Someone should have reported it). Should be reported is about now or later.

  4. Nothing was broken. The back door was simply opened ______ the spare key we keep under the mat.

    • Aby
    • Bwith
    • Cfrom
    • Dof
    Show the answer

    B. with

    Use by for the doer and with for a tool that the doer uses. A key is a tool, so we say opened with the spare key. By is the trap: opened by the key sounds as if the key did the action by itself. With a person, you could say opened by my neighbor with the spare key.

  5. Don't worry about the move. By the time you arrive on Friday, all the furniture ______ and set up.

    • Awill deliver
    • Bwill have delivered
    • Chas been delivered
    • Dwill have been delivered
    Show the answer

    D. will have been delivered

    By the time you arrive on Friday looks back from a future moment, so we need the future perfect, and the furniture receives the action, so it must be passive: will have been + past participle. Will have delivered is the trap: it is active and needs a subject that does the delivering, like the movers. Has been delivered is present perfect, not future.

  6. The lost dog is believed ______ more than ten miles before a farmer found her.

    • Ato walk
    • Bwalking
    • Cto have walked
    • Dto be walking
    Show the answer

    C. to have walked

    The walking happened earlier, before the farmer found her, so after is believed we need the perfect infinitive: to have + past participle. To walk is the trap. A simple infinitive after is believed refers to the present or to a habit, not to an earlier event. To be walking would mean she is walking right now.

  7. My aunt never cuts her own hair. She ______ every six weeks at a salon near her office.

    • Ahas cut it
    • Bhas it cut
    • Cmakes it cut
    • Dhas it to cut
    Show the answer

    B. has it cut

    When someone else does a job for you, use have + object + past participle: has it cut. The order is the whole point. Has cut it is the present perfect, which would mean she cut it herself, and the first sentence says she never does. Makes it cut and has it to cut are not correct forms.

Tips for the test

  • Ask who does the action. If the subject receives it (a letter, a road, a patient), cross out every active choice first. Often only two choices are left.
  • Then find the time clue. Right now points to is being; already, yet or since to has been; by the time + past to had been; by next Friday to will have been.
  • After a modal, look for be or have been. Must be done is about now or later; should have been done is about the past. Review the modals in Modal verbs.
  • Know your irregular participles. A choice like was stole or has been wrote is always wrong. Check the forms on the irregular verbs page.
  • Train your ear. In fast speech, It's been sent and It's being sent sound close. In the free trainer, use Slower and Show the text to check what you heard, and let the mistake box bring back missed questions after 1, 3 and 7 days.

Questions learners ask

Is the passive bad style? A teacher told me to avoid it.

No. The passive is the normal choice when the doer is unknown, obvious or less important than the action: Visitors are asked to sign in. That is why notices, reports and instructions use it so much. In personal emails and stories, too many passives can sound heavy, so use an active verb when you know who did what and it matters. On a grammar test, you only need to recognize the correct form.

Can every verb be made passive?

No. Only verbs that take an object can be passive, so happen, arrive, die, sleep, occur and seem never are: The accident happened at noon. A few verbs that do take an object are still almost never passive, such as have (meaning own), resemble, lack and fit. You can say She has a car, but not A car is had by her.

Is "The window is broken" a passive?

It can be. The window was broken by a ball last night describes an action, so it is a passive. The window is broken often just describes how the window is now, the way an adjective does (The window is dirty). Both use be + past participle, so look at the context: by + a doer, or a time like last night or every day, points to an action.
