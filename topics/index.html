Grammar and vocabulary practice by topic

One page per grammar point: the rules in brief, the mistakes learners make most often, and 15 practice questions with full explanations. Each page links to its lesson and to the complete set of questions in the trainer.

Updated October 18, 2026

Most points lost on the ECL and the ALCPT come from a small number of grammar areas: tenses, prepositions, modal verbs, conditionals, the passive, reported speech and word order. Vocabulary questions add words in context, phrasal verbs, idioms and the everyday language of service life. These practice pages let you work on one of those areas at a time, in about twenty minutes.

How to use these pages

  1. Read the rules in brief. Each page starts with a short summary table. If a rule is new to you, open the full lesson first: each page links to it.
  2. Read the common mistakes. They show the wrong sentence next to the right one. Many test questions are built around exactly these errors.
  3. Answer the 15 questions before you open the answers. Write your choices on paper, then check them. The explanation tells you why the right answer is right and why the most tempting wrong answer is wrong.
  4. Practice the full set in the trainer. The button at the end of each page opens every question on that topic in a new order, and the questions you miss go to your mistake box.

Grammar

  • Verb tenses practice

    Present perfect or simple past, the past progressive and the past perfect, future forms and time clauses: how the time words in a sentence choose the tense.

  • Simple past practice

    Regular and irregular past forms, did and didn't, was and were, and the finished-time words that call for the simple past.

  • Prepositions practice

    At, on and in for time and place, by and until, movement words, and the fixed combinations such as responsible for and interested in.

  • Determiners practice

    A, an, the or no article, countable and uncountable nouns, much and many, few and a few, each and every, either and neither.

  • Modal verbs practice

    Must and have to, mustn't and don't have to, can and be able to, may and might, should, and past forms such as must have and should have.

  • Conditionals practice

    Zero, first, second, third and mixed conditionals, unless and in case, wish and if only, and the formal inversions used in orders and reports.

  • Passive voice practice

    Be plus the past participle in every tense and with modals, when to keep by, the get-passive and forms such as is said to have.

  • Reported speech practice

    How tenses move back, say and tell, reported questions with if and whether, reported commands, and verbs such as offer, warn and deny.

  • Sentence structure practice

    Comparatives and superlatives, as ... as, word order in questions, adverb position and the inversion after hardly, never before and not only.

Vocabulary

  • Phrasal verbs practice

    Separable and inseparable verbs, what the small words up, off and out add, three-part verbs, and the look-alike verbs that tests mix on purpose.

  • Military English vocabulary practice

    Ranks and roles, orders and duty, the 24-hour clock, the phonetic alphabet and the everyday words of base life, in their American usage.

Reference lists

  • 115 American idioms with meanings

    Common expressions from work, money, time and daily life, each with its meaning and an example, with flashcards and a quiz.

  • Irregular verbs list

    112 irregular verbs with their simple past and past participle, grouped by pattern so they are easier to learn, with practice questions.

  • 309 phrasal verbs with meanings

    Every verb with its type (separable, inseparable or no object), its meaning and an example sentence.

  • ECL vocabulary by theme

    Useful words for the ECL grouped by theme, with examples.

Which topic first?

Start with the area where your last practice test cost you the most points. If you have not taken a test yet, take the free ECL practice test first: your score by part tells you where to begin. As a rule, learners gain the most points quickly from tenses and prepositions, because they appear in many questions, and from phrasal verbs and idioms in listening. The four-week study plan puts the topics in a sensible order, and the 30 common grammar mistakes guide is a fast way to review them all before a test.
