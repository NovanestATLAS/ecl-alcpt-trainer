The ALCPT test: format, scores and how to prepare

The placement test of the American Language Course: how many questions it has on the new and older forms, how long it lasts, what the score means, the mistakes that cost the most points and a four-week plan.

Published November 18, 2026 · Also in العربية

The ALCPT, short for American Language Course Placement Test, is the placement test of the American Language Course (ALC), the military English course of the Defense Language Institute English Language Center (DLIELC). DLIELC also writes the ECL. The ALCPT checks how well you understand American English in listening and reading, and with them your grammar and vocabulary. It does not test speaking or writing, and every question is multiple choice with four options.

What the ALCPT is used for

According to the ALCPT handbook published by DLIELC, the test is used to:

  • place a student in the right level of the ALC books;
  • measure progress at the end of each level;
  • check whether a student is ready for the ECL, so that nobody takes the ECL too early;
  • assess the English of some local national employees.

The handbook adds that the ALCPT is not meant to prepare for the ECL or to be used for promotions or rewards. If you are asked to take it, the result will most likely decide your level in the course or when you take the ECL.

How many questions, and how long?

DLIELC changed the format in 2025, so two kinds of test forms are in use today. Ask your test center or your teacher which one you will take.

Forms 151 and later (2025 handbook)Forms 1 to 150 (2023 handbook)
Listening (part 1)50 questions, about 20 minutes, timed by the recording66 questions
Reading (part 2)50 questions in 35 minutes34 questions
Total100 questions, about 70 minutes with the instructions100 questions

In part 1 you hear recorded questions, statements and short dialogs in English, then choose the answer in the test booklet. You cannot slow the recording down or play it again, so practicing listening at a steady pace is the most useful thing you can do before the test. In part 2 you read questions, sentences and short passages and choose the answer. Grammar and vocabulary questions appear in both parts.

What the ALCPT score means

An ALCPT score is not an ECL score. ALCPT scores are tied to the levels of the ALC books: they place you in the right level and show how close you are to the level the ECL asks for. The 2025 handbook states that a score of 29 or below does not show a valid level.

The ECL is the official measure of English level for training decisions. It is scored from 0 to 100 and a result stays valid for 180 days. See the ECL test guide and ECL vs ALCPT.

The percentage you see at the end of the practice tests on this site is not an ALCPT score: it is the share of questions you got right. Use it to follow your progress from week to week.

Mistakes that cost the most points

Many points are lost on a few predictable things rather than on English in general. Here are the most common ones, each with the lesson that fixes it:

  • Words that sound almost the same. ship and sheep, full and fool, pill and bill: only one sound tells them apart. In listening, use the meaning of the whole sentence to decide which word you heard. You will meet these pairs often in the listening questions.
  • Short forms. he's, they're, I'd and won't go by fast in the recordings. If you miss them, you miss the tense or the subject of the sentence.
  • The present perfect. I have lived here since 2020, not I live here since 2020. See the verb tenses lesson.
  • Articles. a and an are easy to drop, and the is easy to add where it does not belong: Life is hard, not The life is hard. See articles and quantifiers.
  • Prepositions after adjectives. afraid of, angry with or at, married to. They are learned one by one: see the prepositions lesson.
  • Questions. English needs do, does or did: Where do you work?, not Where you work? See questions and question tags.
  • Military and everyday vocabulary. Words such as barracks, convoy, mission or shift, and phrasal verbs such as carry out or set up, come back often. Train them with the ALCPT vocabulary test and the phrasal verbs lesson.

A four-week plan

20 to 40 minutes a day is enough if you do it every day. Start each session with your mistake box in the trainer, then follow the plan:

  1. Week 1: find your level. Take the ALCPT practice test (50 questions) and write down your result in each part. Then do one set of listening questions every day.
  2. Week 2: grammar. Pick three weak points from your result or from the list above, read the lesson for each, then answer the matching practice by topic.
  3. Week 3: vocabulary. Take the ALCPT vocabulary test, and learn new words every day from the vocabulary list and the phrasal verbs.
  4. Week 4: full tests. Twice in the week, take a full timed test in the trainer: 50 listening questions heard once, then 50 reading questions, as on the new forms. Take it at the same time of day as your real test.

ALCPT questions people ask

How many questions are on the ALCPT?

100 questions. Forms 151 and later have 50 listening and 50 reading questions. The older forms 1 to 150, still used in some programs, have 66 listening and 34 reading questions.

How long is the ALCPT?

About 70 minutes on the new forms, with the instructions: about 20 minutes of listening, timed by the recording, and 35 minutes of reading.

Can I find real ALCPT tests to practice?

No. DLIELC is the only source of ALCPT material and only authorized officials handle it, so it is not published. You do not need it: the test measures skills you can train with original questions, which is what this site gives you.

Is the ALCPT score the same as the ECL score?

No. The ALCPT places you in the ALC books and shows whether you are ready for the ECL. The ECL is the official measure of your English level for training decisions.
