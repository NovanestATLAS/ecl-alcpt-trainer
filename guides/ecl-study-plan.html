A four-week study plan for the ECL

Twenty to forty minutes a day, built around short sets, listening and your own mistakes.

Updated November 7, 2026

Short daily practice works better than long sessions once a week. This plan asks for 20 to 40 minutes a day for four weeks. Adapt it to your level: if a week feels easy, move on; if it feels hard, repeat it.

Before you start: measure your level

Take a timed practice test of 40 questions. Do not prepare for it. Write down your percentage and the date. You will take the same kind of test at the end of week 2 and week 4 to see your progress.

In the trainer, set your daily goal in Settings > Daily goal. Twenty questions a day is a good start.

Week 1: tenses and prepositions

Most mistakes come from a few patterns: since and for with the present perfect, an action that is interrupted in the past, and fixed prepositions of time and place (at 0800, in July, on Monday). Work on tenses and prepositions. Read every explanation, even when your answer was right.

Week 2: the big structures

Move to conditionals, modals, the passive and reported speech. Add 10 vocabulary questions a day. At the end of the week, take a 40-question timed test and compare it with your first one.

Week 3: words and expressions

Vocabulary questions are quick to answer when you know the word and impossible when you do not. Practice Vocabulary in context, American and military terms, then the American idioms and phrasal verbs. Say each new expression aloud in a sentence of your own.

Week 4: test conditions

Start each day with your mistake box (Review > Smart review). Then take mixed quick tests. Twice this week, take a full timed test of 60 or 100 questions without stopping. Keep the last two days before your test light: review, sleep, and do not try to learn new material.

The grammar lessons, one a day

Some learners prefer to learn a rule before they practice it. If that is you, add one lesson a day during the first two weeks, in the order of our grammar course. Each lesson takes a few minutes to read, shows the common mistakes with their corrections, and ends with a quick check. Do the quick check, then the day's practice set in the trainer.

Days 15 to 28 then belong to vocabulary and test conditions, as in weeks 3 and 4 of the plan. Keep the phrasal verbs and vocabulary by theme lessons open while you practice, and return to any grammar lesson whose topic keeps appearing in your mistake box.

Habits that make the difference

  • Same time every day. Ten minutes before work is easier to keep than an hour once a week.
  • Read the choices first. In listening questions, take a few seconds to read the four choices before the recording starts, then listen for the meaning. You will know what to listen for.
  • Keep a short notebook. Copy the rule from each explanation you got wrong, with one example.
  • Trust the mistake box. A question you got wrong stays in the box. Each correct answer sends it further away (one day, then three, then seven), and the fourth correct answer clears it.
