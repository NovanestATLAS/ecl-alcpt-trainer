ECL vs ALCPT: how the two tests differ

Both tests measure listening and reading in American English, but they do different jobs. Here is what each one is for and how it is built.

Updated October 9, 2026

People often use the names ALCPT and ECL as if they were the same test. They come from the same school, the Defense Language Institute English Language Center (DLIELC), and both measure listening and reading. But they are used for different decisions.

The ECL decides, the ALCPT places and screens

The ALCPT handbook describes the ECL as the primary instrument used for assessing the English language proficiency of international military students. The ECL is a strictly controlled test.

The American Language Course Placement Test (ALCPT) is used inside English language training programs. According to its 2025 handbook, it may be used to:

  • place students in the right level of the American Language Course (ALC);
  • check progress at the end of an ALC level;
  • screen candidates to see if they are ready to take the ECL;
  • evaluate the English of local personnel.

The handbook also says that the ALCPT is not used for ECL practice, promotion or bonus systems. Screening with the ALCPT first means fewer people take the ECL before they are ready.

Who gives the ALCPT

DLIELC is the only source of ALCPT materials, and only authorized officials may handle, store and give the test. Real ALCPT forms are therefore not meant to circulate publicly.

What this means for your preparation

You cannot practice with real test forms, and you do not need to. Both tests reward the same skills: understanding grammar quickly, knowing the meaning of common and military words, and following spoken American English. That is what the trainer works on, with original questions:

  • grammar and vocabulary questions with an explanation for each answer;
  • 5,037 recorded listening questions with a man's and a woman's voice;
  • timed practice tests of 20, 40, 60 or 100 questions.
